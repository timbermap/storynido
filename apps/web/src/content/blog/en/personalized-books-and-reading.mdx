When children hear their own name in a story, something changes: they stop being spectators and become **the hero**. That feeling of "this story is about me" is one of the simplest ways to spark a love of reading.

## They see themselves in the story

Kids love spotting familiar things: their pet, their best friend, their favorite food. Every personal detail is a small surprise that invites them to keep turning pages to discover what else is about them.

## Reading becomes a shared moment

Personalized books tend to be read again and again. That repetition, which can tire grown-ups, is valuable: children anticipate words, recognize sentences and start "reading" from memory, a natural step before reading for real.

## Ideas to make the most of it at home

- **Read together at bedtime.** Point to their name every time it appears and ask them to find it.
- **Invent the next page.** Ask what the hero would do next.
- **Make it a ritual.** The same story at the same time helps end the day calmly.
- **Celebrate progress.** If they recognize a new word, cheer: motivation is the best fuel.

## A keepsake that grows with them

Besides supporting reading, a book with their own story becomes a keepsake of that stage: who they were, what they loved and who they played with. Many parents keep it to read again when their kids are older.
